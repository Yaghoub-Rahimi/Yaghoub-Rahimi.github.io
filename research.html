Research

Research overview

My current research is in analytic number theory, with a focus on primes, additive problems, and arithmetic structure in sparse sets. Discrete harmonic analysis is a foundation and continuing part of this work; additive combinatorics provides tools for understanding how density and distribution lead to additive representations.

This direction grew out of my work on Fourier-analytic estimates for arithmetic averaging operators. The same questions about distribution and cancellation now inform my study of prime sums and restricted-digit patterns. My earlier work in sparse recovery forms a complementary strand in analysis and optimization.

Arithmetic structures and analytic number theory

I study additive representations involving primes using the Hardy–Littlewood circle method and combinatorial arguments. In joint work on higher-order sums of primes, we establish representation results for sufficiently dense subsets of the primes. Our work on Gaussian primes combines harmonic-analytic estimates with Goldbach-type questions in sectors. These problems connect the distribution of primes to the additive structure they contain.

Fourier-analytic and harmonic-analysis methods

My work on discrete harmonic analysis studies averages over primes and primes in arithmetic progressions, including improving estimates and maximal inequalities. Fourier analysis makes it possible to separate arithmetic structure from cancellation and to connect operator estimates with additive number theory.

A related project with Rui Han concerns generalized Minkowski–Funk transforms. Their spectral multipliers lead to a small-denominator problem, linking Diophantine approximation to the endpoint Sobolev regularity of inverse transforms.

Restricted-digit sets, primes, and related problems

Restricted-digit and Cantor-type sets offer a setting in which digital structure interacts with the distribution of primes. In Primes with Restricted-Digit Differences, with Rui Han and Fan Yang, we study prime pairs with restricted-digit differences and three-term arithmetic progressions in primes with restricted-digit common differences.

Under a digit non-resonance condition, a localized Fourier criterion gives asymptotic counting formulas with explicit leading constants. A transfer-operator argument verifies the criterion for several families of digit sets. This work brings together analytic number theory, Fourier methods, and additive patterns.

Earlier work in sparse recovery

My earlier research concerns recovery from undersampled measurements through nonconvex optimization. Joint work develops a scale-invariant ℓ1/ℓ2 model, accelerated numerical schemes, and a lifted ℓ1 framework. These papers remain part of my research record, while my current emphasis is on arithmetic and number-theoretic questions.
